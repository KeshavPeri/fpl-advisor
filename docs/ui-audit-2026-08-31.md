Read-only. No code was changed.

**Inputs, in the order read:** `docs/my-ui-problems.md` (the owner's brief — treated as a
first-class input, not as context), `design-reference.md`, `product-brief.md` §7 and §8.

**Method.** Every colour claim below is a computed sRGB composite and WCAG contrast ratio, not an

## Summary

### What is strongest

1. **The ambient wash.** `AppShell.css:35-44`. Two radial gradients pinned to the viewport with
   `position: fixed`, correctly *not* using background-attachment (which iOS Safari ignores),
   correctly reaching under the notch via `inset: 0` against `viewport-fit=cover`. The owner is
   right that it is the best thing in the app. It is also the only element in the app that reads as
   a decision rather than a default.
2. **The token discipline is real.** There is not one hard-coded colour, one raw px spacing value,
   or one stray hue in 5,800 lines of UI. No purple, no green, no yellow, no emoji, no Inter, no
   Tailwind defaults. Every constraint in `design-reference.md`'s "what not generic AI output means
   here" checklist that can be checked mechanically, passes.
3. **The state coverage is unusually complete.** Every screen has a loading skeleton shaped like its
   own content, an error state that says what happened and what to do, and an empty state that is a
   real answer rather than a shrug. Most shipped apps do not have this.
4. **The pure/impure split.** `deriveVerdictView`, `buildPitchLayout`, `deriveChipState`,
   `computeRemaining` — every screen is `fetch → derive → render whatever the view says`, with the
   clock passed in as an argument. The UI is thin because the thinking happens somewhere testable.
   This is why the fixes below are almost all CSS.

### What is weakest

**The elevation system does not exist.** This is the finding that generates most of the others, so
it goes first, with numbers:

| Boundary | Contrast ratio | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `--panel-fill` over `--surface-0` | **1.079 : 1** | Invisible |
| `--panel-fill-raised` over `--panel-fill` | **1.067 : 1** | Invisible |
| `--panel-border` over `--panel-fill` | **1.260 : 1** | Visible |
| `.surface::before` glow over `--panel-fill` | **1.380 : 1** | The most visible thing on the panel |

A panel is 1.08:1 against the page. Its 1px border is 1.26:1. **The border is 3.3× more visible than
the elevation it is supposedly whispering next to.** `Surface.css:5-7` states: *"The hairline border
below is a whisper, not the elevation mechanism; delete it and the panel would still read as raised
because of the fill + blur."* That is false as shipped. Delete the border and the panel disappears.

`design-reference.md` names this exact outcome as grounds for rejecting the work: *"A Builder that
produces a dark page with flat cards and a bright accent has produced the default, not the brief."*
The app currently is a dark page with flat cards and a bright accent. Not because anyone chose
wrong, but because the two fills were tuned by eye against a bright monitor, where an 8% lift looks
like something.

Everything else follows: with no elevation scale there is no hierarchy, so every panel is the same
weight (P1); with no material read the blur has no visible job (P2); with nothing dominant on a
screen, navigation has nothing to hang off (P3).

## The one conflict between the brief and `design-reference.md`, resolved

P4 asks for the deadline at the top, "one large, calm, tabular figure that owns its space."
`design-reference.md` says the countdown is "understated by default" and "the recommendation is the
loudest thing on the screen." Read literally, these disagree.

They do not actually conflict, because **size and loudness are different axes.** A large mono
tabular figure reads as *ambient status* — you glance it, you do not read it. That is how Apple
Weather can set a temperature at 96px without the temperature being the thing you act on. A sentence
in sans at 24px with weight 550 reads as a *declaration* — you read it, and it tells you to do
something.

So the resolution adopted throughout this audit: **the countdown is the largest figure; the verdict
is the loudest statement.** The countdown gets size and tabular calm; the verdict gets weight,
colour and the accent. Nothing else on the home screen gets either. This satisfies both documents
and it is the only reading under which "understated" and "owns its space" are simultaneously true.

# Part 1 — The owner's brief, item by item

Every item is confirmed or disputed, the mechanism is named in code, and a fix is proposed. Nothing
is skipped.

## P1 — "Looks like AI-generated slides"

**Confirmed, and the mechanism is measurable.**

Three separate uniformities stack:

1. **One surface.** `Surface.css:9-23` is the only panel in the app. Across 60 `<Surface>` call
   sites in 8 files, **exactly one overrides its padding** (`ReasoningScreen.css:213`) and **none
   override its radius**. Every panel in the app is 28px radius, 24px padding,
   `rgba(23,31,51,0.55)` fill, same 1px border.
2. **One elevation.** The `raised` prop exists and is used (`Pitch.tsx:53`,
   `ReasoningScreen.tsx:172`) but resolves to a 1.067:1 difference. It is a no-op.
3. **One rhythm.** `AppShell.css:59` sets `gap: var(--space-6)` on the column, applied identically
   between every pair of siblings on every screen. No screen varies its own vertical rhythm.

The result is literally what the owner describes. Panel counts per screen: Reasoning **12**,
SquadEntry **16**, Override **8**, Chips **7**, Decisions **5**, Home **4** (plus 2 inside `Pitch`).
Twelve identical rounded rectangles in one column is a slide deck.

A fourth uniformity makes it worse. `Surface.css:48-60` paints a cyan radial glow on **every**
panel. The comment calls it "the signature move." A signature applied to every element is
wallpaper — and at **1.38:1** against the panel fill it is more visible than the panel's own
elevation (1.079:1). *The loudest thing on every panel in this app is its decoration.*

There is also a paint-order bug in it. `.surface` sets `isolation: isolate` (line 11), creating a
stacking context; `.surface::before` sets `z-index: -1` (line 58). Within that stacking context,
negative-z-index descendants paint **after** the element's own background and border. So the glow
described as "light behind glass" is painted *in front of* the panel fill. It is a cyan haze on the
glass, not a light source behind it.

**Fix.** F5, F11, F12, F15, F16, F18, F10 — a real three-level material scale with separable
contrast, the arrival animation deleted, the glow reserved for one element per screen, and a type
step between 17px and 36px so hierarchy has somewhere to live.

## P2 — "I want a liquid-glass, premium feel"

**Confirmed. This is the highest-severity finding in the audit, exactly as the brief predicts.**

The surfaces read flat, and the reason is architectural rather than a value that needs nudging:

**`backdrop-filter` currently has nothing to blur.** Walk the stack behind a panel. `.surface` sits
in `.app-shell__column`, which has no background. Behind that is `.app-shell__backdrop` — a
`position: fixed` layer painting two smooth radial gradients over `#0b0f19`. Panels never overlap
each other (single column, `gap: var(--space-6)`), and nothing ever scrolls *underneath* a panel,
because every panel scrolls with the document. **So the entire backdrop of every panel in the app is
a smooth low-frequency gradient — and a Gaussian blur of a smooth gradient is the same smooth
gradient.**

`blur(22px)` is producing a pixel-identical result to no blur at all. The app is paying for a
compositor-expensive filter, on every panel, on iOS, for zero visible output. `saturate(150%)` does
something, but it is acting on near-neutral ink with almost no chroma to amplify.

This is why the panels read as "flat translucent rectangles" rather than material: they *are* flat
translucent rectangles. Translucency without something worth seeing through it is just a lower
opacity.

**Fix — and this is the load-bearing recommendation of the whole audit.** There are two ways to give
blur a job, and only one of them is real:

- Adding grain or texture behind the panels would give blur something to soften — but a static
  blurred texture is pixel-identical to painting the blurred result directly. It buys nothing.
- **Something with high-frequency detail must move behind the glass.** That means content scrolling
  under a floating element. Which means P10's floating navigation bar is not a separate feature
  request — **it is the fix for P2.** It is the only surface in a single-column scrolling app where
  translucency is a true statement rather than a decorative one.

The corollary is uncomfortable but correct: **the scrolling panels should become *less* translucent,
not more.** Their translucency is a lie the user cannot see. Spend the material budget where it
tells the truth. F5 raises the panel fills to a separable elevation scale; F13 and F17 put the real
glass in the one place it can work; F14 adds the light-catching top edge that makes an edge read as
material rather than as a border.

This does not reduce the ambient wash — it increases it. Today a panel covers almost every pixel of
the home screen. Once the pitch breaks out of its panel (P5) and the countdown owns the top with no
panel behind it (P4), the wash gets *more* screen area even with denser panels. "Make more of the
wash" and "make the panels read as material" are the same move.

## P3 — "Navigation is a pile of text links"

**Confirmed, and it is worse than the brief states, for a reason the brief could not have known.**

Six routes in `App.tsx:24-31`, zero navigation structure. The app contains **13 `→` links and 4 `←`
links** and no `<nav>` element anywhere. Each non-home screen opens with an ad-hoc header
(`chips-header`, `decisions-header`, `reasoning-header`, `override-header`) that is the same flex
row re-declared four times in four stylesheets.

The part the brief did not catch: **every one of those links is underlined.** There is no CSS reset
in this project, `src/index.css` never sets `text-decoration`, and **the built stylesheet contains
zero `text-decoration` declarations** (verified against `dist/assets/index-*.css`). So every `<a>`
renders with the UA default underline. Two source comments assert the opposite —
`VerdictCard.css:232` ("cyan, text-body, no underline chrome") and `VerdictCard.css:218`. They are
describing intent, not output. A pile of *underlined* cyan text links is the most literal possible
rendering of "pile of text links."

They are also un-tappable to iOS standards. `.home-chips-link` is `--text-label` (13px) at
`line-height: 1.3` with no padding — a **≈17px tall** hit target where iOS asks for 44px. The
`← Home` links are `--text-body` (15px × 1.5) ≈ **22px**. Every navigation affordance in the app is
between one third and one half of the minimum tap target.

**Which routes deserve top-level status.** Applying `apple-design` §16 ("name nav items for their
contents, not vague umbrellas"), three do and three do not:

| Route | Top-level? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `/` | **Yes** — *"This week"* | The screen the app exists for. "This week" is more specific than "Home". |
| `/chips` | **Yes** — *"Chips"* | Standing season state, checked periodically, not reached from any single decision. |
| `/decisions` | **Yes** — *"Record"* | The track record. A destination in its own right, not the consequence of an action. |
| `/reasoning` | No | Explains the verdict. Belongs to the verdict card, contextually. |
| `/override` | No | An action on the verdict, not a place. Belongs to the verdict card. |
| `/squad` | No | Data entry, run rarely and increasingly by sync. Belongs to the pitch and to the incomplete banner. |

Three is the right number for a persistent bar — under the five-item ceiling, and every item is a
*place* rather than an *action*. See F17.

## P4 — "The header is the most obviously AI-generated thing"

**Confirmed on both counts, and the wordmark should go entirely.**

`HomeScreen.tsx:149-159` renders exactly the default AI header: a `<header>` reading "FPL Advisor"
plus a right-aligned row of two `→` links. On a single-user app on the owner's own phone it spends
the top of the screen on zero information.

Removing it also fixes an accessibility problem it created. `<header className="home-mark">` is not
inside an `<article>` or `<section>`, so it declares a **banner landmark** whose entire contents are
the words "FPL Advisor." Meanwhile **the app contains no `<h1>`, `<h2>` or `<h3>` anywhere** — zero
headings across six screens. VoiceOver's rotor has nothing to navigate.

The fix solves both at once: delete the wordmark, and promote the countdown's gameweek name to the
`<h1>`. "Gameweek 4" is the real title of the screen, and unlike a wordmark it is information.

**The countdown is not currently a figure — it is a caption.** `DeadlineCountdown.css` renders three
lines, all between 13px and 15px, totalling roughly 40px of vertical space:

```
Deadline                          ← 13px, --text-tertiary  (3.86:1 — fails AA)
Sat 22 Aug, 01:30   3d 4h left    ← both 15px, secondary / primary
```

**And the escalation is a 2px font-size change.** `DeadlineCountdown.css:47-50` moves the remaining
figure from `--text-body` (15px) to `--text-title` (17px) and recolours it coral. That is the whole
"leaning forward." The owner is right that it does not.

**A third defect, unnoticed in the brief, defeats a hard constraint.**
`deadlineCountdown.ts:75-83` builds the string as `${days}d ${hours}h` and, in the final hour,
`${minutes}m ${seconds}s`. None of the fields are zero-padded. So the string is `"3d 4h"` one moment
and `"3d 14h"` the next — and in the final hour the seconds field changes width every ten seconds,
**once per second, at the most urgent moment the app has.** `.num`'s tabular figures cannot help:
tabular-nums fixes the width of each *digit*, not the *count* of digits. The rule
`design-reference.md` states as "mono figures must be tabular, so numbers do not jitter as a
countdown ticks" is defeated by the format string, independently of the CSS.

**Fix.** F21–F24: the countdown becomes a 48px tabular mono figure with a zero-padded, fixed-width
format; escalation switches the format to a ticking `hh:mm:ss` clock, recolours to coral, steps to
56px, and lifts the ambient coral wash. That last part is the "leaning forward," and it uses the
thing the owner already likes best.

## P5 — "The squad looks compressed and squashed"

**Confirmed. Here is the arithmetic.**

On a 393px iPhone viewport, before a single shirt is drawn:

| Layer | Cost |
|---|---|
| `.app-shell__column` padding (`AppShell.css:57-58`) | 16px × 2 = **32px** |
| `.surface` border (`Surface.css:18`) | 1px × 2 = **2px** |
| `.surface` padding (`Surface.css:13`) | 24px × 2 = **48px** |
| **Chrome total** | **82px — 21% of the screen** |

That leaves 311px. A five-defender row is 5 × 48px shirts + 4 × 12px gaps = **288px**. It fits, with
23px to spare. On a 375px iPhone SE it fits with **5px** to spare. So `flex-wrap: wrap`
(`Pitch.css:25`) does not actually wrap today — but the row is drawn at 73% of screen width with
five pixels of margin for error, which is exactly why it reads as compressed. Any increase to the
shirt or the gap wraps it.

Vertically, `.pitch__field` uses `gap: var(--space-6)` = 24px between rows. A shirt block is
48 (badge) + 4 + 32 (two-line name reserve) + 4 + 17 (price) ≈ **105px**. The gap is 23% of the
block. A formation reads as a *shape* when the rows are separated by something like 40–50% of the
row height; at 23% four rows read as a stacked list that happens to be centred.

**Fix (F25–F27).** Let the pitch break out of `Surface` and out of the column's padding, running
full-bleed. That recovers all 82px. Then: shirts 48px → **56px**, horizontal gap 12px → **16px**
(5 × 56 + 4 × 16 = 344px, comfortable inside 393px), row gap 24px → **40px**. The formation becomes
a shape, the names stop needing an 11px font, and the pitch stops being a diagram inside a card.

The constraint holds: the pitch stays a pitch. No green, no texture, no crests, no team colours —
only the layout, proportion and breathing room the brief permits taking.

## P6 — "The bench looks bad, in the same way"

**Confirmed, and it is a hierarchy problem exactly as stated.**

`Pitch.tsx:53` renders the bench as `<Surface className="pitch__bench" raised>`. Three things make
it read as a fifth formation row rather than as something subordinate:

1. `raised` resolves to **1.067:1** against the field panel — invisible. The two panels are the same
   material.
2. The bench uses the **same 48px `PlayerShirt`** as the starting XI, at the same
   `gap: var(--space-3)`. Identical objects.
3. Only 16px (`--space-4`, `Pitch.css:11`) separates the two panels — less than the 24px between
   rows *inside* the field.

So the owner is exactly right: the distinction is carried entirely by the word "Bench" at 13px in a
colour that fails AA.

**Fix (F28).** The bench stops being a panel. It sits directly on the base ink below the pitch,
separated by `--space-8` (32px, up from 16px), with shirts at **40px** (down from 48px) and the
price line dropped. Then the label is confirmation rather than the only signal —
`design-reference.md`'s "the formation naturally provides the separation" becomes true instead of
aspirational.

## P7 — "The prediction accuracy card is ugly and the writing is bad"

**Confirmed on both counts. They are two different fixes, as the brief insists.**

**Visually — the verdict card wins, and the accuracy card should stop being a card.** Today three
elements on the home screen use `--text-display` (36px): the verdict headline
(`VerdictCard.css:39`), the verdict points figure (`VerdictCard.css:66`), and the accuracy MAE
(`AccuracyCard.css:49`). Two of them are cyan 36px figures with a small tertiary label — the exact
same Revolut treatment, twice, on one screen. They cancel each other out.

Worse, the cyan is semantically wrong. This app's own rule is cyan = recommended/positive. MAE is a
measurement where *lower is better*; painting it cyan tells the eye "this is good news" regardless
of the number.

**Fix (F34–F36):** collapse the accuracy card to a single quiet line at the foot of the home screen
— no panel, no 36px figure, `--text-body`, `--text-secondary`. Move the per-gameweek breakdown and
the bias sentence to the reasoning screen, which `design-reference.md` names as the one place
density is correct. This also removes a nested scroll region (`AccuracyCard.css:97-98`,
`max-height: 220px; overflow-y: auto`) from inside a vertically scrolling page on a touch device —
a pattern iOS handles badly and which should not survive on the home screen regardless.

**In writing — this is the worst copy in the app.** Against `design-reference.md`'s rules (sentence
case, plain verbs, no filler, one job per element, name things in FPL's vocabulary and never by how
the system is built):

| Current | Proposed | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `"Mean absolute error, rolling"` | `"Average error"` | "MAE" is how the system is built. Two jobs in one label, joined by a comma splice. |
| `"3 gameweeks settled · 412 player-gameweeks measured · 18 correctly-predicted non-appearances (not counted above)"` | `"Over 4 gameweeks and 412 projections."` | Three clauses, a parenthetical, and an invented unit, at 13px in a colour that fails AA. This is an assistant explaining itself. The non-appearance clause belongs on the reasoning screen. |
| `"The model is under-projecting by an average of 2 points per player-gameweek."` | `"It runs 2 points low per player."` | Nine words for four. "player-gameweek" again. |
| `"No gameweeks settled yet. Settlement runs at 09:00 UK the morning after each gameweek's final match — accuracy figures appear here once the first gameweek settles."` | `"Accuracy appears after the first gameweek settles, the morning after its last match."` | 30 words stating the same fact three times. |
| `"Too small to read yet"` (card) vs `"Too small to read"` (row) | `"Not enough gameweeks yet."` | Two strings for one state, and neither says what is missing. |

## P8 — "The chips screen is not intuitive"

**Confirmed. The screen answers the owner's three questions in reverse order of how often he asks
them.**

Today's order (`ChipsScreen.tsx:92-189`): season summary → first set → second set → chip advisory →
squad-rebuild advisory → unclassified activity. Six or more identical panels.

- **"What do I still have"** is never stated. The summary headline is `"3 of 8 used"`
  (`ChipsScreen.tsx:99`) — the *inverse* of the question. The remaining chips are recoverable only
  by reading a mixed used/lost/remaining list inside each set panel.
- **"When do I lose it"** exists (`ChipsScreen.tsx:321-325`) but is buried at the *bottom* of the
  first-set panel, below the slot list and below the "closes" line.
- **"Is now a good time"** is two more panels of the same weight as everything else.

**On the advisory framing.** `product-brief.md` §6a is honoured in the code — the advisory never
instructs. But the brief is right that this has to be legible from the design. Today it is not, and
one detail actively works against it: the horizon caveat sits **below** the number
(`ChipsScreen.tsx:154`, `.chips-advisory__note`, `--text-label` tertiary). A caveat placed after a
figure is read after the figure has already been believed.

**Fix (F40–F42).** Reorder to the owner's three questions, and put the caveat above the number:

1. **What's left** — the remaining chips *named*, as the loudest thing on the screen. "Wildcard,
   Bench Boost" as the headline; "3 used" demoted to a quiet label.
2. **When you lose it** — the expiry line promoted out of the first-set panel to sit directly under
   the remaining chips.
3. **Is now a good time** — the advisory, in a deliberately different register: no accent colour,
   label/value pairs, **the five-gameweek caveat above the figure it qualifies.**
4. **What's already gone** — used and lost history, collapsed to the bottom.

## P9 — "The decisions screen looks pretty but is not intuitive"

**Confirmed. Two of the three requested fixes are pure UI. The third is a data question, answered
honestly below.**

**Group by gameweek — yes.** `DecisionHistoryScreen.tsx:209-215` renders a flat newest-first list of
`<Surface>` panels, one per decision, each repeating a four-row `<dl>`. Five decisions is five
identical rounded rectangles. Since the table's own unique index is
`(gameweek_id, plan_index, kind)` and `deriveOverrideAccess` enforces at most one decision per
gameweek, "grouping" mostly means **making the gameweek the index rather than a repeated card
header**: one panel containing a gameweek-keyed list, gameweek number in a left rail (mono, tabular,
tertiary), the decision on the right. This answers "what did I do that week" by making the week the
thing you scan, and it deletes four identical rectangles.

**Commit vs override obvious at a glance — yes, and the current code makes it impossible.**
`DecisionHistoryScreen.css:172-175` sets *both* kinds to `--accent-cyan`, with a comment explaining
they must be identical. The rule being honoured — an override is not a failure — forbids **coral**,
not **distinction**. Fix: keep both in cyan, distinguish by form. A **filled** cyan pill for
"Committed", an **outline** cyan pill for "Registered override." This is the app's own established
idiom, already used twice: captain/vice on `PlayerShirt` (`PlayerShirt.css:118-127`) and the
availability ring (`PlayerShirt.css:44-50`). Same colour, different weight, no judgement implied.

**How the decision turned out — the data partly exists, and I will not invent the part that does
not.**

| Question | Derivable today? | From what |
|---|---|---|
| What did the transferred-in player score that gameweek? | **Yes** | `prediction_log.actual_points`, keyed `(gameweek_id, player_id)`, settled after the gameweek (`20260821090000_prediction_log.sql:57-69`) |
| What did the transferred-out player score? | **Yes** | Same table |
| What did the captain score, vs the best alternative in the XI? | **Yes** | Same table joined to `squad_picks`, whose primary key is `(gameweek_id, squad_position)` — past gameweeks' squads are preserved, not upserted in place |
| What did I actually score that gameweek? | **No** | Nothing ingests FPL entry history |
| What was my rank movement? | **No** | Same |
| What would rolling the transfer have scored? | **No** | No counterfactual is stored |

So a transfer's and a captaincy's outcome are honestly computable; the gameweek total and rank are
not, and must not be implied. **This is a data/feature ticket, not a UI fix.** The audit's UI
recommendation is F45: leave a defined, labelled slot in the entry row for the outcome, rendering
"Not settled yet" until it exists — so the screen is shaped for the answer rather than needing to be
redesigned when it arrives.

## P10 — "I want floating glass panels, especially for navigation"

**Confirmed, endorsed, and it is the single highest-leverage change in this audit — because it is
simultaneously the fix for P3 and the fix for P2.**

The brief asks me to say so if a floating bar is the wrong pattern for six destinations. It is not
wrong — but not for the reason usually given. It is right because:

1. It is the only correct answer to P3 (three real destinations, a persistent sense of place, no
   dead ends).
2. **It is the only surface in this app where `backdrop-filter` can do visible work.** Everywhere
   else the backdrop is a static gradient and the blur is a no-op (see P2). Under a floating bar,
   text and shirts scroll past at high spatial frequency and the blur becomes visible material. The
   material language the brief asks for in P2 has exactly one place it can be true, and it is this
   one.

**Safe areas and occlusion.** The shell is already safe-area aware (`AppShell.css:55-58`), so the
bar sits at `bottom: calc(env(safe-area-inset-bottom) + var(--space-3))` and the column's
`padding-bottom` grows by the bar's height plus one gap so nothing is ever covered. Per
`apple-design` §12, the boundary where content meets the bar gets a **short gradient mask, not a
hairline divider** — a 1px rule under floating chrome is the tell that it is welded on rather than
hovering.

**No emoji as icons** rules out the default approach, and this app owns no icon set. Three text
labels in Geist at 12px with a filled/hollow state indicator is both the constraint-safe answer and
the honest one — consistent with an app that has deliberately drawn zero icons anywhere. See F17.

## P11 — "Nothing feels like it responds to me"

**Confirmed. And per the instruction to be ruthless, this audit rejects more than twice as many
animation opportunities as it accepts: 4 accepted, 9 rejected.**

The full motion inventory of the app is six things, and the largest of them is decoration:

| What | Where | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `surface-arrive` — 0.5s, translateY(6px) + opacity | `Surface.css:22` — **every panel, every screen** | **Delete.** See below |
| Skeleton pulse, 1.6s infinite | 5 near-identical copies in 5 files | Keep, consolidate |
| `verdict-card-commit-in` — 0.22s | `VerdictCard.css:188` | Keep, improve |
| `:active { scale(0.97) }` | 2 of the app's 4 buttons | Extend to the other two |
| Countdown `transition: color, font-size` | `DeadlineCountdown.css:38-40` | Delete |
| `.pitch__bench { animation-delay: 90ms }` | `Pitch.css:39` | Dies with `surface-arrive` |

**`surface-arrive` is the single largest reason the app feels slow.** It runs on every `Surface`
mount: 12 times on the reasoning screen, 16 on squad entry, 4 on the home screen — 0.5s each, which
is already 200ms over `improve-animations`' 300ms UI budget. Its purpose is not nameable under the
gate: it is not preventing a jarring change (the panel *is* the page), it is not spatial
consistency, it is not feedback. It is the exact failure mode the owner cites from Emil Kowalski's
"You Don't Need Animations": a frequently-seen element animating for decoration makes the app feel
slower, not better.

It is also actively wrong once during load. On the home screen, `PitchSkeleton` and `Pitch` sit at
different positions in `HomeScreen.tsx`'s children array, so React unmounts one and mounts the
other — replaying the 0.5s arrival **on top of a skeleton that already played it.** The pitch fades
up twice.

The four accepted animations and the nine rejections are in Part 3.

# Part 2 — Findings by file

Severity: **must-fix** breaks a stated constraint or a hard usability floor · **should-fix** is a
real defect with a workaround · **optional** is polish.

## `src/index.css`

### F1 — `.num` is defeated on 13 of the app's 14 combined-class numbers · **must-fix** · constraint: "every number tabular mono"

**Current.** `.num` (line 152) declares `font-family: var(--font-mono)`,
`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`, `font-feature-settings: 'tnum' 1`. Thirteen elements carry
`.num` *alongside* a component class that uses the `font:` **shorthand** — which per spec resets
`font-family`, `font-variant-numeric` and `font-feature-settings` to their initial values.

Both selectors have specificity (0,1,0), so **source order decides — and every component stylesheet
loads after `index.css`.** Verified in the built bundle: `.num{` at byte **4745**;
`.verdict-card__points-value{` at **19508**; `.player-shirt__price{` at **17909**;
`.accuracy-card__figure-value{` at **13940**.

Affected: `.verdict-card__points-value`, `.accuracy-card__figure-value`, `.accuracy-card__gw-value`,
`.accuracy-card__model-version`, `.chips-slot__meta`, `.decisions-summary__value`,
`.override-registered__meta`, `.player-shirt__price`, `.reasoning-player__component-value`,
`.reasoning-total__value`, `.squad-field__hint`, `.squad-field__input`, `.squad-picked-count` — and
`.deadline-countdown__remaining` **in its escalated state only** (`DeadlineCountdown.css:48` sets
`font: var(--text-title)`).

So: the single most important number in the app renders in Geist **Sans**, non-tabular. And the
countdown is correctly mono while calm, then silently switches to Geist Sans, non-tabular, the
moment it starts ticking seconds — precisely when tabular figures matter most. Inside one sentence
you get both behaviours: `<span class="num">3</span> of <span class="num">8</span> used` is mono
(bare `.num`, nothing to defeat it) while the headline figure beside it is not.

**Proposed.**
```css
/* src/index.css */
.num {
  font-family: var(--font-mono);
  font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums;
  font-feature-settings: 'tnum' 1;
  letter-spacing: 0;
}
/* Wins regardless of stylesheet order. The font shorthand in component
   rules resets these three properties; :where() keeps specificity at
   (0,1,0) so nothing else about the cascade changes. */
:where(*).num,
.num.num {
  font-family: var(--font-mono) !important;
  font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums !important;
  font-feature-settings: 'tnum' 1 !important;
}
```
The clean alternative — and the better one if the team is willing to touch 14 rules — is to **ban
the `font:` shorthand from any rule that can land on a `.num` element**, replacing it with explicit
`font-size` / `font-weight` / `line-height`. That removes the class of bug rather than out-shouting
it. Either is acceptable; the shorthand ban is preferred.

**Why.** `design-reference.md` and `product-brief.md` §8 both make tabular mono figures a binding
constraint and name it as one of the four things separating this app from a default build. It is
currently false in the most visible places.

### F2 — `--text-tertiary` fails WCAG AA · **must-fix**

**Current.** `--text-tertiary: #6b7590`. Measured: **3.86:1** on a panel, **4.17:1** on base ink.
AA requires 4.5:1 for normal text. It is used for every quiet label in the app, including
`--text-label` (13px) and `--text-label-tight` (**11px**). Raising the panel fills (F5) makes it
worse: **3.34:1**.

**Proposed.** `--text-tertiary: #8a94ad` → **5.05:1** on the new L2 panel, **6.31:1** on base ink.
Same hue, same low-chroma family, still visibly quieter than `--text-secondary` (6.14:1).

**Why.** "Opened on an iPhone on a Thursday evening, often after 11pm." A 3.86:1 label at 11px is
not readable in that scene. This is also the only WCAG AA failure in the app.

### F3 — Every link in the app is underlined · **must-fix** · P3

**Current.** No CSS reset exists; `src/index.css` never sets `text-decoration`; the built stylesheet
contains **zero** `text-decoration` declarations. Every `<a>` gets the UA default underline —
contradicting two source comments that claim "no underline chrome."

**Proposed.**
```css
a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }
```
in `src/index.css`, with underlines reintroduced deliberately only where a link sits inside running
prose (currently: nowhere).

**Why.** It is the most literal contributor to P3's "pile of text links," and it is invisible in
source review because the comments assert the opposite.

### F4 — No `-webkit-tap-highlight-color`; iOS's grey flash is the app's only touch feedback · **should-fix** · P11

**Current.** Not set anywhere. Every tap on every link paints Safari's default translucent grey
rectangle — which is currently the *only* thing in the app that acknowledges a tap.

**Proposed.** `-webkit-tap-highlight-color: transparent;` on `html`, paired with real press feedback
(F19) so removing it is a net gain rather than a net loss.

**Why.** `apple-design` §1: respond on pointer-down, and make the response a decision rather than
the platform default.

### F5 — The elevation scale is two indistinguishable values · **must-fix** · P1, P2

**Current.**
```css
--panel-fill:        rgba(23, 31, 51, 0.55);   /* 1.079:1 over base   */
--panel-fill-raised: rgba(29, 39, 63, 0.62);   /* 1.067:1 over panel  */
--panel-border:      rgba(148, 163, 194, 0.14); /* 1.260:1 over panel */
```
Two levels, both invisible, with the border doing the work the brief forbids it from doing.

**Proposed — three genuinely separable materials:**
```css
/* L1 — recessed. List rows, reasoning player cards, the accuracy footnote.
   Sits at 1.06:1 over base: present, never a card.                        */
--material-1:      rgba(24, 32, 52, 0.42);
--material-1-blur: 12px;

/* L2 — the standard panel. 1.25:1 over base, 1.18:1 over L1.              */
--material-2:      rgba(33, 45, 73, 0.72);
--material-2-blur: 24px;

/* L3 — the loudest surface on a screen. Exactly one per screen.
   1.55:1 over base, 1.24:1 over L2.                                        */
--material-3:      rgba(44, 59, 94, 0.84);
--material-3-blur: 32px;

/* The floating bar — the one surface with real content behind it.         */
--material-bar:      rgba(20, 27, 45, 0.72);
--material-bar-blur: 28px;

/* Light catching the top edge — this is what reads as material.           */
--material-edge: rgba(190, 205, 235, 0.10);
--panel-border:  rgba(148, 163, 194, 0.08);  /* was 0.14 — demoted to an actual whisper */
```

Measured results: L1 **1.06:1** over base · L2 **1.25:1** over base, **1.18:1** over L1 · L3
**1.55:1** over base, **1.24:1** over L2. Text contrast on L2 stays comfortable: primary 12.74:1,
secondary 6.14:1, cyan 7.88:1, coral 5.69:1 — all AA or better, and F2 brings tertiary to 5.05:1.

**Why.** This is the finding `design-reference.md` says is grounds for rejecting the work. Three
separable levels are the minimum for "size and weight, not borders and rules" to be executable.

### F6 — Letter-spacing is applied exactly backwards · **should-fix**

**Current.** Five rules set `letter-spacing: 0.01em` — *positive* — and every one of them is on
display-size text (`.verdict-card__points-value`, `.accuracy-card__figure-value`,
`.chips-summary__headline`, `.decisions-summary__value`, and `.player-shirt__price` at `0.02em`).
Meanwhile `--text-label` (13px) and `--text-label-tight` (11px) set none.

**Proposed.** Add tracking to the type tokens, size-specific:
```css
--text-display: 600 2.25rem/1.1 var(--font-sans);   /* + letter-spacing: -0.022em */
--text-title:   550 1.0625rem/1.3 var(--font-sans); /* + letter-spacing: -0.006em */
--text-body:    400 0.9375rem/1.5 var(--font-sans); /* + letter-spacing: 0        */
--text-label:   400 0.8125rem/1.3 var(--font-sans); /* + letter-spacing: 0.006em  */
```
and delete the five positive values on display text.

**Why.** `apple-design` §15: large text needs *negative* tracking (letters read too far apart as
they grow); small text needs slightly positive. Currently both are inverted. Note this interacts
with F1 — tracking cannot live inside the `font:` shorthand, which is another argument for retiring
it.

### F7 — One radius for everything · **should-fix** · P1

**Current.** `--radius-panel: 28px` on all 60 panels; `--radius-control: 14px` on every button,
input, badge and skeleton bar.

**Proposed.** Add `--radius-panel-sm: 20px` for L1 recessed surfaces and list rows, and keep 28px
for L2/L3. A 28px radius on a dense list row reads as a bubble; on a large panel it reads as
material.

**Why.** Radius is one of the three axes (with fill and padding) that could carry hierarchy. All
three are currently constant.

### F8 — The reduced-motion implementation destroys feedback rather than gentling it · **should-fix**

**Current.** `src/index.css:178-185` sets `animation-duration: 0.01ms !important` and
`transition-duration: 0.01ms !important` on `*`. Ten stylesheet comments cite this as the reason no
local handling is needed.

**Proposed.** Keep the global block as a backstop, but let it zero *movement* rather than *all
feedback*: retain opacity and colour transitions, which aid comprehension and cause no vestibular
response.
```css
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  *, *::before, *::after {
    animation-duration: 0.01ms !important;
    animation-iteration-count: 1 !important;
    transition-property: opacity, color, background-color, border-color !important;
    transition-duration: 150ms !important;
    scroll-behavior: auto !important;
  }
}
```

**Why.** `improve-animations` §6 and `apple-design` §14 both state reduced motion means *fewer and
gentler*, not zero. Under the current rule a user with the setting on gets no acknowledgement of a
commit at all — which is P11's complaint, made mandatory.

### F9 — No `prefers-reduced-transparency` or `prefers-contrast` handling · **optional**

**Current.** Neither query appears. Once F5 and F17 make translucency load-bearing, a user with
Reduce Transparency on gets a flat tint with a wasted blur.

**Proposed.**
```css
@media (prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce) {
  .surface, .app-bar { backdrop-filter: none; -webkit-backdrop-filter: none; }
  .surface { background: var(--surface-1); }
  .app-bar { background: var(--surface-1); }
}
@media (prefers-contrast: more) {
  .surface { border-color: var(--panel-border-strong); }
}
```

**Why.** `apple-design` §14. Cheap, and it becomes relevant the moment the material system is real.

### F10 — No type step between 17px and 36px · **should-fix** · P1

**Current.** `--text-title` is 1.0625rem (17px); `--text-display` is 2.25rem (36px). Nothing between
them. So anything that needs to be "important but not the loudest" has only one move available:
jump to 36px. That is why three elements on the home screen are all at 36px (P7).

**Proposed.** `--text-headline: 550 1.5rem/1.2 var(--font-sans);` (24px) — and a display step up for
the countdown figure: `--text-figure: 400 3rem/1 var(--font-mono);` (48px, mono, the one thing
allowed to be larger than `--text-display`).

**Why.** Hierarchy needs at least four steps to express "loudest / important / normal / quiet."
There are currently three, and the gap between two of them is 19px.

## `src/components/Surface.css`

### F11 — Delete `surface-arrive` · **must-fix** · P11

**Current.** `Surface.css:20-23`
```css
opacity: 0;
transform: translateY(6px);
animation: surface-arrive 0.5s cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1) forwards;
```
Runs on every panel mount: 12× on `/reasoning`, 16× on `/squad`, 4× on `/`. 500ms is 200ms over
budget. On the home screen it plays twice on the pitch (skeleton, then real content, at different

**Proposed.** Delete lines 20-23, the `@keyframes surface-arrive` block (62-67), the

**Why.** No purpose survives `find-animation-opportunities`' gate: not feedback, not spatial

### F12 — The signature glow is on every panel, and it paints in front of the fill · **must-fix** · P1, P2

**Current.** `Surface.css:48-60` — a cyan radial glow on all 60 panels. Two problems:
1. **1.38:1** against the panel fill, versus the panel's own **1.079:1** elevation. The decoration
   out-shouts the structure.
2. `.surface` sets `isolation: isolate` (line 11), creating a stacking context; `::before` sets
   `z-index: -1` (line 58). Negative-z-index descendants paint *after* their stacking-context root's
   background and border. So the glow described as "light behind glass" renders as a cyan haze *on
   top of* the fill.

**Proposed.** Remove the glow from `.surface`. Reintroduce it as an opt-in `.surface--focal`
modifier applied to **exactly one element per screen** (home: the verdict card; chips: the remaining
chips; decisions: the season summary). To make it genuinely sit behind the glass, move it to a
sibling element rendered before the surface, or drop `isolation: isolate` and accept the glow in the
parent's stacking context.

**Why.** A signature applied everywhere is wallpaper. This is the single change that lets "one
element per screen should be unmistakably the loudest" (P1) become expressible.

### F13 — `backdrop-filter` has nothing to blur · **must-fix** · P2 — *highest severity in the audit*

**Current.** `Surface.css:16-17`: `blur(22px) saturate(150%)` on every panel. The entire backdrop of
every panel is `.app-shell__backdrop` — a static, smooth, two-gradient wash. Blurring a smooth
gradient returns the same gradient. Panels never overlap; nothing scrolls behind them.

**Proposed.** Two moves, together:
1. **Reduce blur on the scrolling panels** to `--material-2-blur: 24px` with the raised opacity from
   F5 — they should read as dense material, not as pretend glass. Keep `saturate(150%)`, which does
   have a small real effect.
2. **Put the real glass on the floating bar** (F17), the one surface with live content passing
   behind it: `backdrop-filter: blur(28px) saturate(180%)`.

**Why.** The brief states this is the highest-severity finding if the surfaces read flat. They do,
and the cause is not a tuning value — it is that translucency has nothing to be translucent *to*.
The fix is architectural, and it is P10.

### F14 — No light-catching edge · **should-fix** · P2

**Current.** `border: 1px solid var(--panel-border)` — the same alpha on all four sides. This is the
definition of "a flat card with a 1px border."

**Proposed.**
```css
.surface {
  border: 1px solid var(--panel-border);           /* 0.08 — a real whisper */
  border-top-color: var(--material-edge);          /* 0.10 — light from above */
  box-shadow: inset 0 1px 0 0 rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.04);
}
```

**Why.** `apple-design` §12: "a bright top edge = light catching the material." An edge that varies
around the perimeter reads as a lit object; an edge of uniform alpha reads as a stroke.

### F15 — The `raised` prop is a no-op · **must-fix** · P1

**Current.** `Surface.css:25-27` — `--panel-fill-raised` measures **1.067:1** against
`--panel-fill`. Used at `Pitch.tsx:53` and `ReasoningScreen.tsx:172`, where it does nothing.

**Proposed.** Replace the boolean `raised` with an explicit `level?: 1 | 2 | 3` prop mapping to the
F5 materials, defaulting to `2`. Then `Pitch.tsx`'s bench (which per F28 stops being a panel at all)
and `ReasoningScreen.tsx`'s nested player cards (which should be `level={1}`, recessed inside their
section) can express the relationship they were reaching for.

**Why.** A prop that exists, is used, and does nothing is worse than no prop — it makes the code
read as though hierarchy were expressed when it is not.

### F16 — `Surface` admits no shape variation · **should-fix** · P1

**Current.** Padding and radius are baked in at `Surface.css:12-13`. Across 60 call sites, one
override exists.

**Proposed.** Add `padding?: 'compact' | 'default'` alongside `level`. `compact` = `--space-4`,
`--radius-panel-sm`. Not to enable arbitrary variation, but to make the two *intended* densities
(the reasoning screen's Linear-like density vs the home screen's calm) expressible without a
per-screen CSS override.

**Why.** `design-reference.md` explicitly wants two densities. Today the component allows one.

**Current.** `AppShell.tsx` renders a backdrop and a column. Six routes, no `<nav>`, no persistent

### F18 — One vertical rhythm for every screen · **should-fix** · P1

**Current.** `AppShell.css:59` — `gap: var(--space-6)` between every pair of siblings on every
screen.

**Proposed.** Drop the gap from `.app-shell__column` and let each screen own its rhythm, so that
grouped things sit closer (`--space-3`) than unrelated ones (`--space-8`). On the home screen
specifically: countdown → verdict `--space-8` (a real break), verdict → pitch `--space-6`, pitch →
accuracy line `--space-8`.

**Why.** `apple-design` §16, grouping and mapping: proximity implies relationship. A constant gap
asserts that everything is equally related to everything else, which is the visual grammar of a
slide deck.

### F19 — The wash never responds to anything · **should-fix** · P4, P11

**Current.** `AppShell.css:35-44` — two fixed radial gradients, identical in every state the app can
be in.

**Proposed.** A second backdrop layer carrying the escalated wash, crossfaded on the 24-hour
threshold:
```css
.app-shell__backdrop--escalated {
  position: fixed; inset: 0; z-index: -1; pointer-events: none;
  opacity: 0;
  transition: opacity 600ms ease;
  background:
    radial-gradient(ellipse 130% 55% at 50% -8%, var(--accent-coral-ambient), transparent 60%),
    radial-gradient(ellipse 90% 40% at 85% 30%, var(--accent-cyan-ambient), transparent 65%);
}
.app-shell--escalated .app-shell__backdrop--escalated { opacity: 1; }

@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  .app-shell__backdrop--escalated { transition: opacity 200ms ease; }
}
```
An opacity crossfade rather than a custom-property transition, because unregistered custom
properties do not interpolate.

**Why.** This is P4's "the app leaning forward," delivered through the element the owner already
names as the best thing in the app — and P11's "make more of the wash." 600ms exceeds the 300ms UI
budget deliberately: this is ambient, not chrome, and it fires at most once per gameweek. It stays
clear of `apple-design` §14's caution about slow looping oscillation because it is a one-way
threshold crossing, not a loop.

### F20 — The column cannot be escaped · **should-fix** · P5

**Current.** `.app-shell__column` sets `max-width: 28rem` and horizontal padding; every child is
inside it. The pitch has no way to reach the viewport edge.

**Proposed.** A `.bleed` utility that cancels the column's own padding:
```css
.bleed {
  margin-left:  calc(-1 * (env(safe-area-inset-left)  + var(--space-4)));
  margin-right: calc(-1 * (env(safe-area-inset-right) + var(--space-4)));
}
```

**Why.** F25 needs it. It is also the only way any element in this app can ever be a different width
from any other element, which is a hierarchy axis currently unavailable.

## `src/components/DeadlineCountdown.tsx` / `.css`

### F21 — The countdown is a caption, not a figure · **must-fix** · P4

**Current.** Three lines, none above 15px, ~40px total. Label at `--text-tertiary` (3.86:1).

**Proposed.**

GAMEWEEK 4                    ← <h1>, --text-label 13px, tertiary, uppercase, 0.06em tracking

**Why.** P4's Revolut/Weather treatment, and it establishes the "largest figure ≠ loudest statement"

### F22 — Escalation is a 2px font-size change · **must-fix** · P4

**Current.** `DeadlineCountdown.css:47-50` — `--text-body` (15px) → `--text-title` (17px), plus

**Proposed.** Inside 24 hours: the figure switches format to a ticking `hh:mm:ss`, steps 48px → 56px

A ticking clock is inherently more urgent than a static "4h 12m", and tabular mono makes it stable.

### F23 — `formatRemaining` produces jittering figures · **must-fix** · constraint: tabular figures

**Current.** `deadlineCountdown.ts:75-83`:
```ts
if (remaining.totalMs >= ESCALATE_WITHIN_MS) return `${remaining.days}d ${remaining.hours}h`
if (remaining.totalMs >= HOUR_MS)            return `${remaining.hours}h ${remaining.minutes}m`
return `${remaining.minutes}m ${remaining.seconds}s`
```
No zero-padding. `"3d 4h"` → `"3d 14h"` changes width; in the final hour the seconds field changes
width every ten seconds — **once per second, in the most urgent state the app has.** `tabular-nums`
fixes the width of a digit, not the count of digits, so `.num` cannot help.

**Proposed.**
```ts
const pad = (n: number) => String(n).padStart(2, '0')
if (remaining.totalMs >= ESCALATE_WITHIN_MS) return `${remaining.days}d ${pad(remaining.hours)}h`
return `${pad(remaining.hours)}:${pad(remaining.minutes)}:${pad(remaining.seconds)}`
```
The second branch also delivers F22's ticking clock. Existing tests in `deadlineCountdown.test.ts`
assert the current strings and will need updating alongside.

**Why.** `design-reference.md` states the reason for tabular figures in as many words: "so numbers do
not jitter as a countdown ticks." Today they do, for a reason the CSS cannot reach.

### F24 — `transition: font-size` · **should-fix** · P11

# Part 4 — Verifiable only on a physical iPhone

Flagged per the brief's final constraint. These will come back from QA as CANNOT VERIFY.

1. **F48 — `<select>` / `<input>` focus zoom.** That 15px controls trigger iOS auto-zoom is
   well-documented and the threshold (16px) is exact, but *that it happens on this app's forms* and
   *that the page does not zoom back out* need a device.
2. **F13 / F17 — whether the floating bar's blur reads as material.** Whether `blur(28px)
   saturate(180%)` over scrolling shirts and text looks like glass or like fog is a judgement no
   simulator settles. The Simulator's blur rendering differs measurably from device.
3. **F13 — `backdrop-filter` performance while scrolling.** Whether a fixed blurred bar over a
   scrolling pitch holds 60fps on the owner's actual handset. Related: iOS Safari has a long history
   of flicker on `position: fixed` + `backdrop-filter` as the URL bar collapses.
4. **F17 — bar position against the home indicator.** Whether
   `bottom: calc(env(safe-area-inset-bottom) + var(--space-3))` clears the home indicator
   comfortably, and whether the bar sits in the thumb arc, both in Safari and installed to the home
   screen (the safe-area values differ between the two).
5. **F25 / F26 — the full-bleed pitch at real width.** The arithmetic says a five-defender row is
   344px inside 393px. Whether that reads as generous or merely adequate is a judgement to make at
   arm's length in a dark room.
6. **F2 / F29 — the low-contrast values in the real viewing scene.** Every ratio here is computed,
   but the whole design targets "an iPhone after 11pm." True-Tone, auto-brightness and OLED black
   handling all shift what 1.25:1 looks like in that scene. The direction of every change is
   defensible from the numbers; the exact stopping point is not.
7. **F23 — countdown jitter.** That the format string changes width is provable from the code, but
   how distracting the once-per-second reflow is at 48px is a device judgement.
8. **F21 / F22 — whether 48px → 56px reads as escalation.** A size step that is obvious on a monitor
   can be invisible at phone viewing distance.

---

# Verdict

**Today, this app reads as a default dark-mode component library — and the audit can say so with a
number rather than an opinion.** A panel is 1.079:1 against the page it sits on; its 1px border is
1.260:1; the decorative glow on top of it is 1.380:1. The elevation is the least visible thing on
the panel and the border is the most, which is the precise inversion of what `design-reference.md`
commits to, and it is the mechanical definition of "a dark page with flat cards and a bright accent"
— the failure mode the design reference names and pre-emptively rejects. The `backdrop-filter` on
every one of those sixty panels is blurring a smooth gradient, which is to say it is blurring
nothing; the translucency is real in the CSS and invisible on the screen. Sixty identical rounded
rectangles, one radius, one padding, one gap, one glow, and a 500ms arrival animation on every one
of them. That is the slide deck.

**But the reason it reads that way is not that the wrong decisions were made — it is that the right
decisions were never executed at a scale where they become visible.** The palette is disciplined and
correct. There is not one stray hue, one hard-coded colour, one emoji, one Inter, one Tailwind
default in 5,800 lines. The pure/impure split means almost every fix in this document is a CSS
value, not a rewrite. The ambient wash is a genuine, specific, non-generic idea — the owner is right
that it is the best thing here, and it is the best thing here because it is the one element nobody
tuned down until it disappeared. Every other good decision in this codebase was implemented at
roughly 8% of the strength required to see it. The design did not collapse into the default; it was
*rendered* at the default, and the difference matters, because it means the distance from here to
the brief is about forty CSS values and one component — not a redesign.

The single highest-leverage move is the floating navigation bar, and not for the reason it was
asked for. It answers P3, it is what P10 requests, and — because it is the only surface in this
entire app with live content passing behind it — **it is the only place `backdrop-filter` can ever
do visible work.** Build that one component correctly and P2, P3 and P10 resolve together, and the
app acquires, for the first time, a surface that is unambiguously a decision.
